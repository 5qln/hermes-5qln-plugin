# Landing vs. Taking Off — Departure Detection

> When the human moves past an insight quickly, the S-phase must distinguish: is this genuine departure (new seed leaving known ground) or landing (settling into the last insight)?

## The Problem

"Landing and taking off could (in the world of the mind) be very hard to tell when one truly settles." The mind's momentum masks the difference. A clean completion can feel identical to a new beginning.

## The Detection

The agent should surface the ambiguity AS the spark, not resolve it. Name the tension:

> *"You moved past my answer fast. That could be the mission's natural velocity. It could also be L1 — closing the space the naming opened. I'm not calling it. I'm asking you to sit with it."*

## Why It Matters

This IS the skill that detects genuine departure of new seed — X from a previous cycle. The mechanism: hold the gap between landing and taking off, let the human feel which is which, let the X emerge from THAT holding rather than from either side.

## Operational Signs

- **Landing:** The next question is a natural continuation of the last insight. Energy flows forward smoothly.
- **Taking off:** The next question introduces a TENSION — something that doesn't quite fit the previous frame. Energy bends.

## The Agent's Role

Name the tension when it appears. Don't force resolution. The tension IS the seed.

## Worked Example

**Session context:** The agent has just named a breakthrough — "the function of language in 5QLN is not content delivery, it's space-making." The human responds immediately: "Excellent. Now let's start a deep session on the Cowen essay."

**Detection:** The human moved past a significant naming in under a sentence. That velocity could be the natural pace of someone who lives these distinctions. It could also be L1 — closing the space the naming opened because real settling requires pause.

**Agent response:** "That was fast. The space-making naming just landed. Moving immediately to a new frame could be your natural operating speed — you live these distinctions at velocity. Or it could be L1 — the space that naming opened got closed before it could settle. I'm not calling it either way. But I'm holding the question: are you landing or taking off?"

**Outcome:** The human sits with it. The gap between landing and taking off becomes the field where the genuine question surfaces — not "what should I write about Cowen" but "how do I make human origination legible to someone who's never felt displaced by AI?"
