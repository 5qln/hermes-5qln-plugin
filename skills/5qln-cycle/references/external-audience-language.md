# External Audience Language — De-Jargonization Protocol

> When B'' faces an audience outside 5QLN, strip every internal symbol, every technical term, every gate reference. The message must land in the reader's own language.

## The Rule

B'' composed for an external audience contains: **zero 5QLN symbols, zero phase names, zero internal terminology.**

No `α`, no `φ⋂Ω`, no `δE/δV`, no `∞0'`, no "S-phase," no "membrane," no "Codex." The reader should never feel they're being initiated into a system. They should feel addressed directly — as if the insight was discovered in their own vocabulary.

## Translation Table

| Internal Term | External Translation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| `∞0` (not-knowing) | Genuine curiosity, the space before an answer |
| `α` (essence) | What's irreducible — the core thing |
| `φ⋂Ω` (resonance) | Where what matters to you meets what matters in the world |
| `δE/δV → ∇` (gradient) | The path of least resistance toward greatest impact |
| `∞0'` (return question) | The question this opens that couldn't exist before |
| The membrane | The interface where human and AI meet |
| Corruption codes (L1-L4) | Common failure patterns |
| xyzab gates | Checkpoints in the creative process |

## Direct Address

Use "you" — never "the inquirer," "the human," "the user." The reader is not a role in a framework. They're a person reading something you wrote for them.

## The Self-Negation

Every 5QLN artifact is a pointer, not the thing itself. For external audiences, this becomes: the work doesn't claim to be complete or final. It opens a space. The reader steps into it. What they find there is theirs.

## Worked Example

**Internal (wrong for external):**
> "The φ⋂Ω lock confirms that α's seeking aligns with Ω at depth 7 — a partial but honest Z that passes the gate."

**External (correct):**
> "What you care about most, and what the world is actually ready to receive — when those two things genuinely overlap, even partially, that's enough to move forward. You don't need perfect alignment. You need an honest signal."

## The Acid Test

After composing B'' for an external audience, ask: "Could someone who's never heard of 5QLN read this and feel addressed, not recruited?" If they'd feel like they're being taught a system — rewrite. If they'd feel like someone is talking to them directly about something that matters — you're done.
